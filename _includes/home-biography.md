## Background

Originally from Thailand, I hold a B.S. in Bio and Brain Engineering, with a minor in Biological Science, from KAIST.

My earliest biological interests grew through aquarium fish, birds, gardens, and observing living organisms in ordinary surroundings. Fishkeeping and a memorable encounter with a male olive-backed sunbird helped turn that curiosity into closer attention to biodiversity.

I attended a POSN astronomy camp in middle school, which sent me into a period of amateur stargazing and astrophotography—tracking satellites and later watching meteor showers. In high school, I progressed through POSN Biology Camp 2 in March 2017, encountering the breadth of biology while developing my interests in ecology and evolution.

After three rounds of national training camps, I was selected as a Thai national representative for the International Earth Science Olympiad in 2018. I participated as part of Team B and was therefore not eligible for an official individual medal, although my individual score fell within the silver-medal range.

At Kamnoetvidya Science Academy (KVIS) in Wang Chan Valley, Rayong, I founded a birdwatching club. A molecular docking workshop at the school introduced me to a different scale of biology: proteins as nanoscale objects that could be visualized and explored on a computer. Jason Chin’s 2018 seminar at the neighboring Vidyasirimedhi Institute of Science and Technology (VISTEC) on genetic code expansion also fascinated me with the possibility of experimentally extending something as evolutionarily conserved as the genetic code.

After graduating from KVIS, I interned at VISTEC with Chayasith Uttamapinant, where I worked on protein characterization for CRISPR diagnostics and metagenomic mining for novel plastic-degrading enzymes. In early 2020, I also took a semester-long leave of absence from KAIST to help establish a CRISPR diagnostics platform in Thailand, which led to my first contribution to a scientific publication. I later returned to VISTEC as a research assistant in 2024, working on microscopy image processing and analysis for protein phase-separation systems.

## A wider biological perspective

> "We are the first generation of scientists with the tools to address the dimensions of biodiversity on Earth…and ironically we may be the last generation with the opportunity to discover and understand Earth’s biodiversity before it is irrevocably changed or lost."

*— James P. Collins, 13 February 2009, as recorded in the [National Research Council symposium report](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/13109/chapter/3), p. 11 (2011).*

That idea resonates strongly with how I think about biology. The molecular tools now available to us make it possible to study biological diversity at extraordinary resolution, but understanding that diversity also requires paying attention to organisms, their environments, and their evolutionary histories.
